The hackathon's first criterion is Agentic Memory Design and the tie-break over the five is

**The ten seconds are a subtraction of two columns, not a sentence.**
`materialised_at − started_at = 10.0 s`, both ISO-8601 off the wire, computed in the artefact
and stated nowhere in the program that produced it
[src: `evidence/demo/memory-loop.json#gap.seconds`, `#gap.stated_anywhere_in_this_program` = `false`].
Both endpoints are corroborated against the checked-in seed — `AGREES` at
`verticals/mainline/db/seeds/demo/demo_permit.sql:250` and `:321` — because the running
database and the committed seed are two independent artefacts.

**Where the `4` came from, every time it appears.** The seed supplies `0` and `routine`
(`demo_permit.sql:318`, in a comment that says so). `mainline.fn_check_project` overwrites both
from `mainline.clause_blame_current` under invariant MI25, welded by
`verticals/mainline/db/migrations/0120_trg_check_project.sql` (BEFORE INSERT, FOR EACH ROW).
**Nobody typed the four** — and that sentence is only true because the projection ran, so the
projector is named beside the number wherever it appears
[src: `evidence/demo/memory-loop.json#projection`].

**The loop closes on itself, and the artefact checks that it does.** Twenty-three cross-response
identities are asserted and all twenty-three hold — the blame edge's `commit_id` is the
obligation's `commit_id`; the blame edge's `event_id` is the obligation's `precursor_event_id`;
the obligation's `recall_run_id` is the run that found it; the receipt line's `check_id` is the
obligation's. A loop that did not close would be four unrelated rows shown in a suggestive order.

## The two screens are real clients of the real kernel

The film is not a tour of the MAINLINE console. It is shot inside the software the people in
the story use, and MAINLINE refuses *inside* it.

| screen | who is using it | route | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permit to work** | a site supervisor issuing a permit | `/operator.html#/permit` | `verticals/mainline/apps/console/src/operator/permit/**`, registered by `route.ts` |
| **Management of change** | a safety engineer editing a clause | `/operator.html#/change` | `verticals/mainline/apps/console/src/operator/change/**` |

`operator.html` is a **second HTML entry point in the same Vite build**, not a page inside the
console — `verticals/mainline/apps/console/operator.html`, routed by
`src/operator/route.ts` (hash routing, two modules, no library). No vendor mark appears on
either screen; you see MAINLINE by seeing what it stops.

**If a judge opens devtools they see a real request and a real answer.** That is the whole
point and it is checkable from outside: `live-beats.json` records the eleven requests and
their `x-amzn-requestid` values, and `#request_discipline` records the rule they were taken
under — every `GET`, one `POST /v1/demo/gate-run`, and one documented trap. Nothing else.

**The trap, recorded once and labelled.** `POST /v1/permits/{permit_id}/merge` on the seeded
subject answers **`423`** `demo_subject_write_protected`, `use_instead: POST /v1/demo/gate-run`.
It carries **no SQLSTATE, no constraint and no MUS** and it is `is_a_gate_refusal: false`.
It is in the transcript so that no screen ever wires an ISSUE button to it: a `423` rendered in
a refusal banner would be a fabricated exhibit
[src: `evidence/demo/live-beats.json#documented_traps[0]`].

## What this page does not prove

Read this before you use any row above as more than it is.

1. **Nothing here is about a browser.** `live_beats.py` and `memory_loop.py` are HTTP clients;
   no browser ran. That a screen renders these bytes is a separate claim with separate
   evidence. `live-beats.json` says so first, at `#not_proven_by_this_transcript[0]`.
2. **The world is synthetic.** Fictional operator, fictional site, fictional people, fictional
   incident. The payload declares it: `SYNTHETIC — recalled precursor DEMO-INC-0001 reaches the
   clause version this permit relies on`. The *mechanism* is real; the inputs are authored.
3. **`wall_ms` is not a service level.** It is one machine's path to `ap-southeast-1` at one
   hour with an unknown warm state. The transcript records the client clock, the server's own
   `elapsed_ms` and the per-beat durations separately and **never adds them**, so a reader can
   see the gap rather than be told about it.
4. **Beat 4's WebAuthn assertion is synthesised** and the envelope declares `staged`. This
   deployment has no authenticator and nothing in the schema verifies a signature; only the
   projected columns on that row are real.
5. **One subject was driven, once.** The transcript names it. It is not a claim about every
   subject or every hour.
